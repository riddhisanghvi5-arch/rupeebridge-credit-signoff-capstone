Task 2: Privacy vs. Security Risk Matrix (2 x 2)High Privacy / High Security: Customer KYC Documents, Credit-Bureau Scores, Loan Application Database, AI Call Recording Archive.   Low Privacy / Low Security: Public Interest-Rate Card.   Low Privacy / High Security (Integrity): Aggregated Monthly Approval Statistics.   Managerial Recommendations:KYC Documents and Call Recordings: Apply end-to-end encryption at rest and in transit with strict access role controls.   Credit-Bureau Scores: Pseudonymize data and enforce audit logs for access.   Application Database: Implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) and row-level access permissions.   Public Rate Card: Ensure public web integrity to prevent unauthorized tampering.   Aggregated Statistics: Restrict edit permissions to prevent data manipulation while allowing internal reporting.  

Task 3: Incident Classification(a) Hacker copies application database: Security Failure (leads to a privacy breach).   (b) Employee sells call recordings: Privacy Failure (unauthorized processing/misuse).   (c) Laptop with unencrypted scores stolen: Security Failure (physical device loss without encryption).   (d) Scores shared with partner bank without consent: Privacy Failure (lack of valid legal basis/consent).   (e) Customer loan data used to target ads: Privacy Failure (purpose limitation violation without consent).  

Task 4: GDPR vs. DPDP Comparison and International ComplianceComparison Details:Dimension 1: ApplicabilityGDPR (EU / Germany): Applies to processing data of EU residents (NRIs in Germany).   DPDP Act (India): Applies to digital personal data processed within India.   Dimension 2: Legal BasesGDPR (EU / Germany): Consent, Contract performance, Legitimate interests, Legal obligation, etc.   DPDP Act (India): Consent and certain legitimate uses (specified ground exemptions).    Dimension 3: Sensitive DataGDPR (EU / Germany): Explicit extra protections for special category data (e.g., biometric, religion).   DPDP Act (India): Does not define separate sensitive tiers; standard digital personal data rules apply.   Dimension 4: RightsGDPR (EU / Germany): Right to erasure (be forgotten), portability, access, rectification.   DPDP Act (India): Right to correction, erasure, grievance redressal, nominating representative.   Dimension 5: Cross-Border TransfersGDPR (EU / Germany): Requires adequacy decision, Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), or explicit safeguards.   DPDP Act (India): Allowed unless specifically restricted by government notification (blacklisting).   

Three Actions for RupeeBridge:Implement granular consent management for NRIs in Germany under GDPR standards.   Establish a lawful cross-border transfer mechanism for German NRI applicant data.   Enforce strict data minimisation by deleting non-essential fields prior to storage.
